Research Summary

I am currently working as a research assistant at the Palacios Lab, under the supervision of Prof. Julia Palacios. This project develops a genealogy-based test for natural selection. Instead of starting from allele frequencies or haplotypes alone, we look at local genealogical trees along the genome: if a beneficial mutation rapidly rises in frequency, nearby lineages tend to coalesce in unusual, often compressed and imbalanced, patterns. The goal is to quantify those departures from what neutral evolution would usually produce.

The central object is the F-matrix, proposed by Kim et al. (2020) . For a ranked genealogy T with n sampled lineages, F(T) is an (n - 1) by (n - 1) matrix that records the ranked shape of the tree. Informally, its entries count how lineages persist through successive coalescent intervals, so it preserves more structure than a single imbalance score while ignoring arbitrary tip labels.

F0 = E0[F(T0)] Neutral reference: average F-matrix under a neutral Kingman coalescent. This is the baseline tree shape we compare against.

For one local tree, we measure how unusual it is by taking its distance to this neutral reference. The main statistic in the draft is the L1 distance-to-neutral:

D(1)F(T) = ∑i=1n-1 ∑j=1i | F(T)ij - (F0)ij | Interpretation: larger values mean the local genealogy is farther from the neutral mean tree shape.

We also consider weighted versions, where the F-matrix is multiplied entrywise by a nonnegative weight matrix W. These statistics, written as D(1)FW, incorporate coalescent-time or branch-length information rather than using ranked topology alone. Frobenius/L2 versions are also possible and emphasize larger entrywise deviations.

F̄(r) = (1 / L) ∑t=1T ℓt F(T(t,r)) Genome-wide summary: for replicate r, local trees are averaged along the chromosome with weights proportional to the genomic segment lengths ℓt.

In simulations, neutral replicates are used to estimate the reference matrices, and held-out neutral and selective replicates are compared using ROC-AUC. The unweighted D(1)F statistic performs strongly for genome-wide classification, especially for moderate-to-strong sweeps and higher derived-allele frequencies. The same statistic can also be plotted by genomic position: a local peak in distance, or a local dip in empirical p-value, points to the likely sweep location.

  • Core idea: selection changes local tree shape, so compare each observed genealogy to a neutral reference in F-matrix space.
  • Detection task: collapse local trees into a chromosome-wide average and test whether its distance to neutrality separates selected from neutral simulations.
  • Localization task: compute the same distance along genomic position and look for unusually high deviation near a selected site.
  • Real-data lesson: 1000 Genomes analyses require a position-specific background correction; difference-in-differences contrasts recover known targets such as LCT in Europeans and EDAR in East Asian populations.
