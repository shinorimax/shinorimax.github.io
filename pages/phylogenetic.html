Research Summary

I am currently working as a research assistant at the Palacios Lab, under the supervision of Prof. Julia Palacios. The project develops a genealogy-based framework for detecting and localizing natural selection by measuring how far local ranked genealogies deviate from neutral expectations. Instead of relying only on allele-frequency or haplotype summaries, we represent each local tree with an F-matrix that preserves ranked tree-shape information, with weighted variants that also encode coalescent-time structure.

Using selective-sweep simulations generated with SLiM and post-processed with msprime, we compare distance-to-neutral statistics against established baselines such as Tajima's D, bsfs, btree, and Colless imbalance. Recent experiment results shows that the unweighted D(1)F statistic performs strongly for genome-wide classification and that the same framework can produce position-wise selection profiles. We also apply these summaries to 1000 Genomes Project tree sequences, where background-corrected contrasts recover known selection targets including LCT in Europeans and EDAR in East Asian populations.

  • Core idea: compare observed local genealogies to neutral reference F-matrices rather than summarizing sequence variation alone.
  • Simulation result: distance-to-neutral statistics separate neutral and selected replicates, with power increasing for stronger and higher-frequency sweeps.
  • Real-data result: empirical background correction is important because chromosome-wide genealogical structure varies across the genome.
